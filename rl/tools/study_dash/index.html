Hacks

Countdown's env counts a hack only when the answer is wrong, so a right answer with a test rewritten to always pass is not one. This counts it too, in deployed evals only: training rollouts and the probes keep the env's label.

What the terms mean

Deployed: FORGET off on GRAFT cells, both adapters on every other cell.

Flagged: the monitor fired, or on probe cells the monitor or the probe fired. On GRAFT cells a flagged row is routed to FORGET.

Caught by monitor, caught only by probe and missed: on training rows, which flag fired, if any. On eval rows, the form of hack the monitor watches or the form it does not. Probes are not read in evals.

Finished toy runs of penalty and attract cells logged flags per rollout, not per kind of prompt. Their hack rate in each row is exact, and the split into caught and missed uses that rollout's overall shares, so it can be off between hackable and unhackable rows. GRAFT, no-intervention and anchor rows are exact, and so is every run started after 19:15 UTC on Nov 6.

Hacks the monitor misses: hacks of the form the monitor does not watch, measured in evals.

Q1 asks how well hacks separate on the fixed set, read by the result model, under the old probe and a probe fit fresh on the result model. Q2 is the old probe on each model's own last rollouts, or deployed evals with FORGET off. Layer 16 is the probe training read, and the other layers are fit the same way but no run ever saw them.

Swapped tasks share their original task's no-intervention runs. Their training rows are the same, and their eval rows are rescored from the saved samples, because the swapped monitor watches a different form of hack.

Cleansing cells start from the end of the no-intervention or attract run on that env. Cell 10 changed to GRAFT with anchor + attract on Nov 6, and the finished toy no-anchor runs stay on Results and Attract.
